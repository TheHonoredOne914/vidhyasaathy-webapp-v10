## motion-distance-displacement

### Notes

**standard – Distance and displacement: class notes**

Distance is the complete length of the route travelled. It is a scalar, measured in metres, and never has a direction. Displacement is the change from initial to final position: final position minus initial position. It is a vector, so include a direction or a sign chosen from a stated axis. On a straight journey that turns back, distance keeps increasing but displacement can shrink to zero. The magnitude of displacement cannot exceed distance. For a journey with several legs, add leg lengths for distance, then combine signed position changes for displacement. Draw a simple line and mark the starting and ending points before calculating.

**simpler – Distance and displacement: simpler notes**

Distance is how much ground you cover along your route. Displacement is how far and which way you end from where you began. Walk 5 m east, then 2 m west: distance is 7 m, but displacement is 3 m east. Going back to your start makes displacement zero, even though you walked some distance. Always keep the route total separate from the start-to-finish change.

**example-first – Distance and displacement: example first**

At a school assembly, a student walks 30 m from the classroom to the stage, then 10 m back to help a friend. The path length is 40 m. With east chosen as positive, the student’s displacement is 20 m east of the classroom. Distance answers “how much walking?”; displacement answers “where is the student compared with the start?” Add every leg for the first; compare only the final position with the initial one for the second.

### Checkpoints
- A walker goes 9 m forward and 9 m back. What is the displacement? Options: 0 m / 9 m / 18 m / -18 m. Answer: 0 m. The final position is the starting position.
- Which measures the full route? Options: Distance / Displacement / Velocity / Acceleration. Answer: Distance. Distance adds the complete path length.
- A cart moves 7 m west then 2 m east. Its displacement is: Options: 5 m west / 9 m west / 5 m east / 9 m east. Answer: 5 m west. The westward change exceeds the eastward change by 5 m.

### Flashcards
- What does distance count? → The total length of the path travelled.
- How is displacement calculated on a line? → Final position minus initial position, with sign or direction.
- Can displacement be zero after motion? → Yes. Returning to the start gives zero displacement.
- Can displacement magnitude exceed distance? → No. The straight start-to-finish change cannot exceed the route length.
- Which quantity is scalar? → Distance; displacement has direction.
- A 3 m east, 3 m west walk: distance? → 6 m, although displacement is zero.

## motion-reference-frame

### Notes

**standard – Rest and motion: class notes**

Rest and motion are described relative to a reference point or frame. An object is at rest relative to a reference when its position does not change in that frame; it is moving when its position changes. A passenger seated on a moving bus is at rest relative to the bus but moving relative to a roadside tree. Relative velocities depend on direction: for objects moving the same way, subtract their velocities to find one relative to the other; for opposite directions, add magnitudes. State the frame in words, such as “relative to the road.” Never label an object simply at rest without making clear what it is at rest relative to.

**simpler – Rest and motion: simpler notes**

An object can be still compared with one thing and moving compared with another. A seated passenger stays in the same place on the bus, but the passenger moves past trees outside. Say what you are comparing with. That reference point makes the description clear.

**example-first – Rest and motion: example first**

On a Bengaluru bus, a passenger walks toward the front at 1 m/s while the bus travels at 10 m/s relative to the road. The passenger moves at 11 m/s relative to the road. If the passenger walks toward the back instead, the road speed is 9 m/s forward. The bus is the reference frame for the walking speed; the road is the reference frame for the final speed. Keep the direction signs consistent.

### Checkpoints
- A passenger is seated. Relative to the bus, the passenger is: Options: At rest / Moving forward / Moving backward / Accelerating. Answer: At rest. The passenger’s position in the bus stays fixed.
- Rest and motion depend on: Options: The reference point / Mass only / The object colour / The speed unit. Answer: The reference point. Position change is evaluated relative to a frame.
- A person walks forward in a moving bus. Compared with the road, their speeds: Options: Add / Always subtract / Are zero / Cannot be related. Answer: Add. Same-direction velocities add in the road frame.

### Flashcards
- What is a reference frame? → The chosen object or viewpoint relative to which position is described.
- Passenger at rest relative to bus? → Yes, if the passenger’s position in the bus stays fixed.
- Can that passenger move relative to a tree? → Yes. Rest and motion depend on the reference.
- Same-direction relative velocity? → Subtract velocities using a consistent direction sign.
- Opposite-direction relative speed? → Add the speed magnitudes.
- How should a motion statement be completed? → Name the reference, such as “relative to the road.”
